YOUR SIMPLE AUSTRALIA DICTIONARY

Important words.
What they mean and how to use them.

New words can be confusing. Open a topic to learn what it means, how to get started and which official website can help.

PLAIN ENGLISH EXPLANATIONS

Learn the basics
one word at a time.

Requirements can change and may depend on your visa or situation. Use the official links to check the latest details.

🚆Opal cardA card used to pay for public transport in Sydney.＋

What it is: A reusable transport card for eligible trains, buses, ferries, light rail and metro services in Sydney and parts of NSW.

How to get one: Buy one from an Opal retailer, such as some convenience stores and newsagents, or check whether contactless payment with a bank card or phone is easier for you.

How to use it: Add value if using an Opal card, then tap on and tap off where required. Keep enough balance for your trip.

Official Opal information ↗
💳Bank account and debit cardA place to keep money and a card to pay with it.＋

What it is: A bank account holds your money. A debit card lets you pay in shops or online using money in that account.

How to open one: Compare banks, apply through the bank's official website or app, and follow its identity-check steps. You may be asked for your passport, visa details, Australian address and other documents.

Important: Each bank has its own rules. Never share your PIN, password or one-time security code.

Australian government banking guide ↗
📱SIM card or eSIMConnects your phone to a mobile network.＋

What it is: A SIM or eSIM helps your phone use mobile calls, texts and data.

How to get one: Compare providers and plans, check your phone is unlocked and supports the SIM type, then follow the provider's activation steps. Identity checks may be required.

Australian communications information ↗
🧾TFN — Tax File NumberA number used for tax and work records.＋

What it is: A personal number used in Australia's tax and superannuation systems.

How to apply: Use the Australian Taxation Office's official guidance for your situation. The application method depends on your circumstances.

Keep it private: Only provide it when there is a legitimate reason, such as to your employer or financial institution.

ATO TFN information ↗
🧑‍💻ABN — Australian Business NumberA number for eligible businesses and activities.＋

What it is: An identifier for a business. Not everyone doing casual work needs an ABN.

How to apply: Check whether you are entitled to one and apply through the official Australian Business Register. Be careful of websites charging for a free government application.

Official ABN application information ↗
🔐myGovA secure account to access some Australian government services.＋

What it is: One online account that can link to supported government services.

How to start: Visit the official website and follow the sign-up steps. Use your own email and create a strong, unique password.

Create or access a myGov account ↗
🩺OSHCOverseas Student Health Cover.＋

What it is: Health insurance cover for many international students in Australia.

How to arrange it: Check your student visa conditions and your education provider's instructions. Confirm what your policy covers before you need care.

Study Australia health cover guide ↗
🚨Emergency number 000Call for police, fire or ambulance in an emergency.＋

When to call: Call 000 when someone is in immediate danger or there is a serious emergency.

What to say: Ask for police, fire or ambulance, explain what happened and give your location as clearly as you can.

Official Triple Zero information ↗
🏠Bond and rentMoney you may pay when renting a home.＋

Rent: The regular payment for living in a property.

Bond: Money paid as security under a rental agreement. Rules and bond lodgement processes differ by state or territory.

Before paying: Read the agreement, keep receipts and check the rental rules for your state or territory.

NSW renting guide ↗
🚌Public transportBuses, trains, ferries, trams and metro services.＋

How to plan: Use your city's official transport website or a maps app to check routes, fares, service changes and travel times.

Paying: Options differ between cities. Check whether you need a local travel card or can use a contactless bank card.

Transport for NSW ↗
🎓Student visa conditionsRules attached to your permission to study in Australia.＋

What it means: Your visa may have conditions about study, work and keeping your details up to date.

What to do: Check your own visa details and current official rules instead of relying only on social media or advice from friends.

Check visa details and conditions ↗

MINDSET / 01

Be ready to
grow through it.

If you are used to living with family or having many things handled for you, life abroad can feel unfamiliar. You may need to cook, budget, travel and solve everyday problems independently. That is a skill you can build — not something you must master overnight.

  • Expect some uncomfortable firsts.
  • Ask for help before a small problem becomes a big one.
  • Protect your sleep, money, boundaries and wellbeing.
  • Give yourself time to adjust and stay connected to people you trust.
CONNECTION / 02

Find your
people.

Your university is a good place to meet people. Try a club, event or study group. You can say, “Hi, I’m new here. Where is a good place to eat nearby?”

  • Try one campus activity each week.
  • Practise English in everyday conversations.
  • Learn local slang gradually; it's okay to ask what something means.
  • Choose friends who respect your goals and boundaries.
PLAN AHEAD

Common challenges.
Practical responses.

01Feeling lonely or homesick+
Keep regular contact with family, build a routine, get outside and attend low-pressure social activities. If your feelings persist or affect daily life, talk to your university's student support service or a qualified professional.
02Everything feels new or uncomfortable+
Learn one route, one shop and one practical skill at a time. Save important addresses offline, ask questions and give yourself permission to be a beginner.
03Budget pressure and unexpected costs+
Track spending, understand rent and bills, keep a small emergency buffer if possible, and contact your university early if money is affecting your study or housing.
04Unsafe situations or risky peer pressure+
Trust your instincts, set clear boundaries, avoid getting into vehicles with impaired drivers and leave situations where you feel unsafe. Call 000 in an emergency. You can enjoy social life without alcohol or drugs.
05Photo ID and getting a licence+
After you settle in, check your state or territory's official transport authority for proof-of-age/photo ID options and the correct learner-driver pathway. In NSW, the learner process includes the Driver Knowledge Test (DKT); requirements vary by situation, so use official guidance.
000
Keep safety simple.

Australia's emergency number is 000 for police, fire and ambulance. Save your accommodation address and a trusted local contact. If you feel unsafe, move towards a staffed public place where possible.
